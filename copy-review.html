The rounds

What each pass actually did

ROUND 01

Write — five agents in parallel

  • Game notes rewritten to answer a question rather than label a title. Each one now tells you whether it suits your group, which the player count beside it cannot.
  • Reviews made to sound like three people. They read as one person in three moods before — now an organiser, a clipped regular quoting figures, and a balanced four-star who names the deduction.
  • Station blurbs given distinct character. Three of the four were interchangeable one-liners; each now says what it is like to sit there, including Bay 05's honest trade — you hear the counter phone.
  • All twelve decided items applied, including "An FC", the Library/Games mismatch, and the tense in the bays standfirst.
ROUND 02

Review — content editor and website reviewer

  • Editor: confirmed no banned word, exclamation mark or emoji survives anywhere in app/, components/ or content/, and the About page holds first person singular throughout. Flagged three voice seams and one comma splice.
  • Site reviewer, the finding that mattered: the two most convincing sections on the page — the bays and the reviews — ended with no call to action at all. The buyout never divided ₹1,200 by 10 seats. The passes never showed a break-even. The Saturday bracket did not appear on the home page.
ROUND 03

Revise — findings applied

  • Every section now ends somewhere. The buyout, pass and bracket arithmetic is computed from the pricing data rather than typed, so it cannot drift when a rate changes.
  • Home page headings now carry figures, so a phone skim reads a ladder: 5 screens → 12 discs → ₹90 to ₹250 → 4.7 from 23.
  • Self-contradictions removed: "Refundable" against the 24-hour rule, "every number below is per hour" against the passes, cake against "food from the counter only".

Caught after the rounds

Three the agents missed, now fixed

The verdict named these and none of the three revision agents had claimed the files. I applied them by hand.

  1. The site quoted two different prices for an hour. lowestRate is the cheapest bay, not the cheapest hour, so ₹120 was the "from" figure in seven places — meta description, hero, the sticky bar on every phone screen, contact, the 404, the share image — while the rates page said ₹90. It was advertising the lounge as a third more expensive than it is. There is now one derived cheapestHour, quoted with its condition attached.
  2. "12 am" survived all three rounds. Every agent declared lib/hours.ts another owner's file. The hours table said "Friday 1 pm — 12 am" four lines above prose saying "run to midnight". One line changed; it now says midnight.
  3. Headsets were free and ₹20 at the same time. The About spec sheet listed them as bay kit; the pricing page charged for them. On a site whose pitch is no surprises at the counter, that was the wrong thing to be vague about.

Still open

What I did not change

Two loose ends, both needing your answer

  • Can 10-hour pass hours be spent at the weekend? Nothing on the site says. The agents were right not to invent an answer.
  • The pricing page headings still carry no figures. "Pay ahead, pay less" sits above ₹999, ₹649 and ₹2,199 and names none of them — the one section that did not get the scannability treatment.

Verdict · round 3 · ships

The words are now ahead of the facts they describe.

Nothing in the copy blocks a launch. What blocks a launch is that the venue name, address, phone number, rates, hours and all three reviews are still invented — and the reviews in particular back the rating markup in the structured data, so they have to be real Google reviews before this goes live, not merely for honesty.

Build passes, TypeScript clean, and the four pages score 95–98 on performance with 100 for accessibility, best practices and SEO.
